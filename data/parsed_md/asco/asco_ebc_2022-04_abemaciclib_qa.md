## Adjuvant Abemaciclib Plus Endocrine Therapy in the Treatment of High-Risk Early Breast Cancer: ASCO Guideline Rapid Recommendation Update Q and A

Rachel A. Freedman, MD, MPH 1 ; Stephanie L. Graff, MD 2 ; Mark R. Somerfield, PhD 3 ; Melinda L. Telli, MD 4 ; Antonio C. Wolff, MD 5 ; and Sharon H. Giordano, MD, MPH 6

In 2020, Johnston et al 1 reported the primary results from monarchE, a phase III, open-label randomized clinical trial, following the second preplanned interim analysis after 19 months of median follow-up. These results showed that the addition of 2 years of abemaciclib, a cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitor, to standard adjuvant endocrine therapy (ET) significantly improved invasive-free survival (IDFS) in the intent-totreat (ITT) population of patients with hormone receptor (HR)-positive, human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-negative, node-positive early breast cancer at high risk of early recurrence. In 2021, Harbeck et al 2 reported absolute improvements in 3year IDFS and distant relapse-free survival rates of 5.4% and 4.2%, respectively, for the ITT population in the final prespecified IDFS analysis for study participants receiving abemaciclib. The US Food and Drug Administration (FDA) then approved adjuvant abemaciclib, but limited the approval to the subset of patients with a worse prognostic risk on the basis of a higher anatomic stage (cohort 1) and with higher tumor proliferation based on Ki-67 biomarker expression by immunohistochemistry.

In a Letter to the Editor following the decision by FDA, the investigators reported overall survival (OS) data on the basis of the first interim OS analysis. 3 Although the OS data were immature, the observed hazard ratios for OS of 1.091 (95% CI, 0.818 to 1.455) for the ITT population and 0.767 (95% CI, 0.511 to 1.152) in the FDAapproved population appear to have influenced the label indication. In a Rapid Communication to Journal of Clinical Oncology, 4 the FDA offered the following statement regarding the current approval in the United States for adjuvant abemaciclib for a subgroup of the ITT population on the monarchE study: "While the benefit risk profile was favorable for the indicated subpopulation (ie, statistically significant improvement in invasive disease-free survival [IDFS] for patients with HR1, HER2-negative, node-positive, early breast cancer [cohort 1] with Ki-67 score $ 20% at the final IDFS analysis with a hazard ratio of 0.643 [95% CI, 0.475 to 0.872; P 5 .0042]), given the immaturity and potential OS detriment, it was not favorable for the ITT population."

To offer a prompt response to these updated analyses, 2 ASCO published a rapid recommendation update of ASCO's selection of optimal adjuvant chemotherapy and targeted therapy guideline 5 to address the use of abemaciclib in the adjuvant setting (Table 1). 6 This companion Q and A article addresses some of the questions that clinicians face as they implement monarchE trial results and the rapid recommendation update into clinical practice.

## Question: Whom Are You Prescribing Adjuvant Abemaciclib to in Clinical Practice?

ASCO's rapid update (January 20, 2022) recommended that 2 years of adjuvant abemaciclib plus ET may be offered to patients with HR-positive, HER2negative, node-positive early breast cancer with a high risk of recurrence, consistent with the monarchE study ITT population inclusion criteria, namely, patients with either $ 4 positive axillary lymph nodes or 1-3 positive axillary lymph nodes and either grade 3 disease, tumor size $ 5 cm, or Ki-67 index $ 20%. This recommendation was consistent with the trial design and was broader than the FDA indication, which was limited to a subset of patients from Cohort 1 ($ 4 nodes or 1-3 nodes if tumor $ 5 cm or grade 3) with Ki-67 $ 20%.

Although we acknowledge the rationale behind the FDA indication, the ASCO rapid recommendation update 6 suggested broader consideration of abemaciclib for the entire ITT population, given the similar, statistically significant relative reduction in risk of relapse observed with abemaciclib in patients with both Ki-67-high and Ki-67-low tumors (despite different prognostic profiles and differences in absolute benefit for 3-year IDFS-7.1% among Ki-67-high cohort and 4.5% among Ki-67-low population) and because of the challenges in the universal clinical application of Ki-67. As follow-up data mature for patients on monarchE, we will update the ASCO guideline, as appropriate.

## Question: How Should We Be Using Ki-67 When Considering Abemaciclib?

At this time, the Ki-67 selection required by the FDA approval excludes a cohort of patients expected to have a particularly high anatomic risk of recurrence, namely, patients with $ 4 lymph nodes involved (N2 or N3 disease). These patients were particularly enriched in the Ki-67-low cohort, with 55% of all patients with $ 4 positive lymph nodes in monarchE being Ki-67-low. Although follow-up data remain incomplete for the ITT population, the improvement in IDFS to date was statistically apparent for the entire monarchE ITT population who received abemaciclib, including those with Ki-67-low and Ki-67-high tumors, as above. There are potential challenges in implementing Ki-67 testing into routine clinical practice because of the observed variability and limited standardization in Ki-67 scoring across centers. 7,8 Consequently, the ASCO rapid recommendation update considered the full ITT population results that showed benefit of abemaciclib across patients with varying Ki-67 scores. We have recommended that adjuvant abemaciclib may be offered per monarchE in those with HR-positive breast cancer with either $ 4 positive lymph nodes (regardless of Ki-67 score) or 1-3 lymph nodes with high-risk features (grade 3 disease or tumor $ 5 cm or Ki-67 $ 20%), as per the study's ITT inclusion criteria, recognizing Ki-67 may not be readily available or implemented.

## Question: What Is the Optimal Timing for Initiation of Adjuvant Abemaciclib and Can Abemaciclib Be Administered With Radiation?

The monarchE trial enrolled patients after a maximum of 16 months from surgery to random assignment, focusing on those who recently completed active therapy (. 95% received neo/adjuvant chemotherapy and radiation). monarchE eligibility also allowed up to 12 weeks of ET at the time of random assignment. This timing of abemaciclib initiation in the trial closely aligns with clinical practice, allowing sufficient time for patients to complete recommended adjuvant chemotherapy and radiation.

It is important to note that the monarchE trial does not evaluate the benefit of adding abemaciclib to ET for patients who are longer into their adjuvant ET course and who would not have been eligible for the study. In clinical practice, any administration of abemaciclib should ideally be offered in a similar context and timing to administration in the monarchE study. Patients receiving radiation and chemotherapy on monarchE all completed these treatments before random assignment and treatment with abemaciclib; for these reasons, and for toxicity concerns, all other therapies should be completed, and the only therapy offered concurrently with abemaciclib should be ET.

## Question: If a Patient Has BRCA1 or BRCA2 Mutation, Can We Combine Abemaciclib and Olaparib in HighRisk Patients?

For patients meeting criteria for monarchE but who also have a germline pathogenic or likely pathogenic variant (PV) in BRCA1 or BRCA2, dual or sequential therapy with olaparib was not examined in the trial. The OlympiA trial 9 separately established the significant IDFS benefit of administering one year of adjuvant olaparib after surgery in those with BRCA1 or BRCA2 PVs and high-risk disease. Although outcome data are not available for patients in monarchE with germline BRCA1/2 PVs, the extent of IDFS benefit in OlympiA (8.8% at 3 years) appears higher than that observed in monarchE to date (5.4% in the ITT) and may be worth prioritizing; management of these patients should be decided on an individualized basis. The potential benefits and toxicities of sequential or concurrent therapy with olaparib and abemaciclib are not known, and there are no data available combining a cyclin-dependent kinase 4/6 inhibitor and Poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase inhibitors. Trials are ongoing (eg, ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT03685331).

## Question: Is There a Preference for Which Endocrine Therapy Agent to Combine With Adjuvant Abemaciclib in Practice?

In the monarchE trial, the type of ET administrated was left to the treating clinician, with 68.3% of patients receiving aromatase inhibitor (AI) therapy and 31.4% receiving tamoxifen as initial therapy, including 14.2% and 7.6%, respectively, in combination with ovarian suppression. Although the benefits of differing ET partners was not a focus of monarchE, a notably higher risk of venous thromboembolism (VTE) was observed when abemaciclib was combined with tamoxifen (any-grade VTE 5 4.1%, grade $ 3 VTE 5 2.2%) compared with AIs (any-grade VTE 5 1.7%, grade $ 3 VTE 5 0.9%). Because of these observed events, clinicians should include VTE risk assessment when deciding on a treatment regimen with patients, perhaps favoring AI use in patients receiving abemaciclib if VTE is of concern.
